# Public API, narrower access tokens, webhooks and templates

GLOSSARY: *Jeton d'accès*, *Webhook*, *Modèle*. Three features shipped together (web 0.44.0); a fourth, two-factor authentication (issue 173), is deliberately not part of it (last section).

## Access tokens

Personal tokens already existed (`api_tokens`, scope `api`, `Authorization: Bearer looty_...`) with the full rights of their owner. They now carry **access** (`read` or `write`), an optional **expiry** and an optional list of **workspaces**. Existing tokens keep what they had (write, all workspaces, no expiry).

Decisions:
- A token acts as its owner and never exceeds them: role checks stay in each route. Limits are applied by one middleware (`token_guard.rs`) before routing, so no handler can forget them.
- A token never reaches `/api/user/tokens*`, `/api/user/deletion*` and `/api/auth/*` (except `/api/auth/me`): otherwise a read-only or workspace-limited token could mint a wider one or ask for the account's deletion.
- Read-only means only GET, HEAD and OPTIONS pass.
- A token limited to workspaces is **deny by default**: only routes whose workspace is known pass (`/api/workspaces/{id}/...`, and boards, columns, tasks, labels and pages, looked up in the database). Routes with no workspace (mail, settings, study, personal…) and routes the guard does not know are refused, so a route added later stays closed to limited tokens until someone teaches the guard about it. `GET /api/workspaces` is filtered to the token's workspaces.
- Tokens for CalDAV (app passwords) and calendar feeds are a different scope and are untouched.
- The raw token is shown once; only its SHA-256 is stored (unchanged).

## OpenAPI

`GET /api/openapi.json` is generated from a table in `openapi.rs` listing the routes Looty promises to keep stable for integrations (workspaces, boards and tasks, documents, calendar events, announcements, webhooks, templates). A test calls each listed route on the real router and fails if one does not exist, so the specification cannot describe a removed route. Other routes work (the interface uses them) but may change without notice. We did not annotate every handler with a derive macro: the table is short, reviewed with the routes, and costs no dependency.

## Webhooks

Per workspace, managed by workspace administrators, never in a personal space. Events: `task.created`, `task.updated`, `task.completed`, `page.updated`, `event.created`, `announcement.published`.
- Personal items are never sent (ADR 0002): events about a task or page with `private_to` are dropped before the queue.
- Payloads carry identifiers and short fields (title, dates, assignees), not descriptions or document bodies; the receiver fetches the rest with a token if it needs it.
- Each delivery is a row of `webhook_deliveries` (queue and log at once, kept 30 days): it survives a restart. Signature `X-Looty-Signature: sha256=HMAC-SHA256(secret, "<timestamp>.<body>")` plus `X-Looty-Timestamp` and a unique `X-Looty-Delivery`, so receivers can reject replays and duplicates. Delivery is at least once.
- Retries after 1 min, 5 min, 30 min, 2 h, 12 h (six attempts), then the delivery is abandoned; ten abandoned deliveries in a row switch the webhook off. At most 1000 pending deliveries per webhook.
- The secret is generated by the server, shown once, and stored encrypted with the instance key (`crypto.rs`); it can be replaced.
- **Outgoing calls are an SSRF risk.** Safest default: `https` only and no private, loopback or link-local address, checked when the webhook is saved and again, with the resolved addresses pinned for the connection, at every delivery (so a DNS change cannot point it inside the network). No redirects are followed. A self-hoster who wants n8n on the same LAN sets `WEBHOOKS_ALLOW_PRIVATE=true`, which also allows `http`.
- Not available in the demo.
- Known gap: `page.updated` is sent when a page is saved through the app or the API (title, icon, place, content), not for each keystroke of live collaborative editing, which the collaboration service writes straight to the database. Reporting those would need the collaboration service to call the backend (debounced); left for later.
- `task.completed` is sent when a task's `is_completed` goes from false to true, remembered in memory; after a restart, the first update seen on an already completed task can send it once more.

## Templates

A template is a board (columns, labels, open top-level tasks with sub-tasks and label names) or a document (title, icon, sanitised HTML) in `workspace_templates`; creating a board or document accepts `template_id` (a template's id, or the name of a provided template).
- Provided templates (Project tracking, Retrospective, Meeting notes, Course notes, Lab report) are code, in French or English by the person's language; they cannot be edited or deleted.
- Only workspace administrators save or delete templates; members who can create boards (MANAGE) or documents (EDIT) see and use them. Saving copies content to everyone who can create, so it is limited to people who can already read everything in the space.
- Personal boards and pages are refused as sources (ADR 0002). People, dates, completion and reminders are never copied.
- An unknown template, or one of another workspace, answers as not found.

## Two-factor authentication: not built

Issue 173 asks for TOTP for **local accounts**, and depends on issue 162 (local accounts), which does not exist: Looty still signs people in only through OpenID Connect and documents that choice. A second factor on top of nothing would protect no login, and designing the sign-in step (secret storage, recovery codes, the "required for administrators" rule that issue 162 itself imposes, Android and desktop code entry) before the password flow exists would be guessing. It should be built together with, or right after, issue 162; the pieces already here that it can reuse are `crypto.rs` for the TOTP secret and `token_guard.rs` for refusing tokens on the enrolment routes.
